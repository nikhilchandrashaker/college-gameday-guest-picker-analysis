Are the guest pickers any good?

Across 2,554 graded picks, guests chose the winner 61.5% of the time. Nobody with a real sample stands clearly above that line, and the luckiest and unluckiest records look like what chance alone produces.

Best of all time depends on how many picks you demand

No guest has more than 45 recorded picks, so thresholds of 50 and 100 picks leave nobody on the board. At 25 picks, Joe Namath and Joel McHale lead at 21–10 (67.7%). Lower the bar to 15 picks and Alex Rodriguez tops it at 20–4. The whisker on each bar is the 95% range of plausible true skill; every one of them reaches the dashed line, the guest average.

Exhibit 2 · Steve Young's perfect appearance

Ten for ten is rare, and it has happened before

At 61.47% per pick, ten straight correct picks has a 0.77% chance, about 1 in 130. Seventy-five appearances in the archive had exactly ten picks, and two of them were perfect: Bubba Watson in 2013 and Steve Young in 2026. Luck alone would give 0.6 of those, so two is plausible (an 11% chance). Alex Rodriguez went 12 for 12 in 2017, the longest perfect slate on record.

Exhibit 3 · Skill or luck

Guest records spread out exactly as luck predicts

Each dot is one guest. The funnel marks where a 61.5% picker would land 95% of the time at each sample size. Small samples swing wildly, which is why 100% and 10% records appear. 13 of 220 guests fall outside the funnel, and chance alone puts about 11 there. Among guests with 25+ picks, spread is smaller than luck alone predicts (p = 0.80).
